🔊 Sound — ध्वनि

Concept notes, key terms and examples

1. Sound is Produced by Vibrations | ध्वनि कंपन से उत्पन्न होती है

Sound is produced when an object vibrates. A vibrating object transfers energy to nearby particles; these particles pass the disturbance forward.

ध्वनि: किसी वस्तु के कंपन से उत्पन्न होती है। कंपन आसपास के माध्यम के कणों में ऊर्जा स्थानांतरित करते हैं।

Vibrating source
कंपन स्रोत→Medium particles
माध्यम के कण→Sound reaches ear

Examples / उदाहरण

  • Plucked guitar string — तार का कंपन
  • Drum membrane — ढोल की झिल्ली
  • Vocal cords — स्वर तंत्रियों का कंपन

2. Sound Needs a Medium | ध्वनि को माध्यम चाहिए

Sound travels through solids, liquids and gases. It cannot travel through a vacuum because there are no particles to transmit vibrations.

ध्वनि ठोस, द्रव और गैस में चलती है; निर्वात में नहीं चल सकती।

3. Longitudinal Waves | अनुदैर्ध्य तरंगें

In air, sound travels as longitudinal waves: particles vibrate back and forth parallel to the direction of wave travel. Regions of compression and rarefaction are formed.

Compression
सघनन↔Rarefaction
विरलन↔Compression

4. Amplitude, Frequency and Time Period

  • Amplitude (आयाम): Maximum displacement from the mean position. Greater amplitude generally means louder sound.
  • Frequency (आवृत्ति): Number of vibrations per second; unit hertz (Hz). Higher frequency means higher pitch.
  • Time period (आवर्तकाल): Time for one vibration; unit second (s).
Frequency = Number of vibrations ÷ Time (Hz)   |   T = 1/f

5. Loudness and Pitch | प्रबलता और तारत्व

Loudness is related mainly to amplitude and is measured in decibel (dB). Pitch depends on frequency. A shrill sound has high frequency; a deep sound has low frequency.

6. Audible Range | श्रव्य सीमा

Most humans hear approximately 20 Hz to 20,000 Hz (20 kHz). Below this is infrasonic; above this is ultrasonic sound. Hearing range varies with age and individual.

7. Human Ear | मानव कान

  • Pinna collects sound.
  • Ear canal directs sound to eardrum.
  • Eardrum vibrates; three tiny bones amplify and transmit vibrations.
  • Inner ear converts vibrations into nerve signals; auditory nerve carries them to brain.

8. Noise Pollution | ध्वनि प्रदूषण

Unwanted or excessively loud sound can disturb sleep, concentration and hearing. Reduce noise by maintaining vehicles, avoiding unnecessary horns, using silencers, planting trees and keeping volume low.

Remember: Sound travels generally fastest in solids, slower in liquids and slowest in gases (for comparable conditions). It does not travel in vacuum.

💡 Light — प्रकाश

Reflection, images, multiple reflection and eye

1. Light and Reflection | प्रकाश और परावर्तन

Light enables us to see objects. We see most objects when light from them or reflected by them enters our eyes.

प्रकाश हमें वस्तुओं को देखने में सक्षम बनाता है। वस्तुओं से परावर्तित प्रकाश आँखों में आने पर वे दिखाई देती हैं।

2. Laws of Reflection | परावर्तन के नियम

  1. Angle of incidence equals angle of reflection (∠i = ∠r).
  2. Incident ray, reflected ray and normal lie in the same plane.
Incident ray↘Normal ⟂ Mirror↗Reflected ray

3. Regular and Diffused Reflection

  • Regular reflection: parallel rays reflect in one direction from a smooth surface; clear image may form.
  • Diffused reflection: rays scatter from a rough surface. It still obeys laws of reflection; a clear image usually does not form.

4. Multiple Reflection | बहु-परावर्तन

Light reflects more than once. Periscopes use mirrors/prisms to see over obstacles. Kaleidoscopes create patterns through multiple reflections.

5. Image in a Plane Mirror | समतल दर्पण

  • Virtual and erect image
  • Same size as object
  • Image distance behind mirror equals object distance in front
  • Laterally inverted (left-right reversal)

6. Human Eye | मानव आँख

  • Cornea and lens focus light.
  • Iris controls pupil size and the amount of light entering.
  • Retina receives image; optic nerve sends signals to brain.
  • Blind spot is where optic nerve leaves the eye.

7. Care of Eyes | आँखों की देखभाल

  • Read in adequate light and at a comfortable distance.
  • Do not look directly at the Sun or intense light.
  • Eat vitamin-A-rich foods, such as carrots, leafy vegetables and other balanced foods.
  • Keep screens at a suitable distance; take breaks.
  • Consult an eye specialist for vision problems.
Key fact: A plane mirror forms a virtual image that cannot be obtained on a screen.

🔥 Combustion & Flame — दहन एवं ज्वाला

Combustion, fire control, fuels and flame zones

1. Combustion | दहन

Combustion is a chemical process in which a substance reacts with oxygen and releases heat, often light.

दहन वह रासायनिक प्रक्रिया है जिसमें पदार्थ ऑक्सीजन से अभिक्रिया कर ऊष्मा और प्रायः प्रकाश उत्पन्न करता है।

2. Conditions for Combustion | दहन की आवश्यक शर्तें

  • Fuel (ईंधन)
  • Oxygen/air (ऑक्सीजन)
  • Heat to reach ignition temperature (प्रज्वलन ताप)
Fuel+Oxygen+Heat→Fire

3. Ignition Temperature | प्रज्वलन ताप

The lowest temperature at which a substance catches fire is its ignition temperature. Different substances have different ignition temperatures.

4. Types of Combustion

  • Rapid combustion: burns quickly when flame/heat is supplied, e.g., LPG.
  • Spontaneous combustion: catches fire without an external flame under suitable conditions, e.g., some materials can self-heat.
  • Explosion: sudden combustion with heat, light, sound and rapid gas release.

5. Fire Control | आग बुझाना

Fire can be controlled by removing any one of the three requirements: cooling below ignition temperature, cutting off oxygen, or removing fuel.

  • Water cools many ordinary fires; do not use water on electrical or oil fires.
  • CO₂ or dry powder extinguishers are suitable for many electrical/oil fires, as directed by the extinguisher label.
  • Never pour water on burning oil; switch off heat if safe, cover with a suitable lid/fire blanket and call for help.

6. Flame Zones | ज्वाला के क्षेत्र

  • Inner dark zone: unburnt vapours; least hot.
  • Middle luminous yellow zone: incomplete combustion; glowing soot particles; moderately hot.
  • Outer blue/non-luminous zone: complete combustion; hottest zone.

7. Fuel and Calorific Value

A fuel releases useful energy when burnt. Calorific value is the heat produced by complete combustion of 1 kg of fuel; commonly expressed in kJ/kg.

8. Ideal Fuel | आदर्श ईंधन

  • High calorific value
  • Moderate ignition temperature
  • Easy and safe to store/transport
  • Burns cleanly; little smoke or harmful residue
  • Affordable and readily available

9. Pollution from Burning

Incomplete combustion may produce carbon monoxide (poisonous), soot and smoke. Burning fossil fuels can release carbon dioxide and other pollutants. Use cleaner fuels, efficient appliances and proper ventilation.

Safety: If a fire is spreading, alert adults, evacuate, and call emergency services. Never attempt to fight a large fire.
